The Wiki That Holds All Information

Background

This project was created as a wireframe and interface redesign inspired by Wikipedia, one of the most recognized knowledge platforms in the world. Wikipedia's strength has always been access to information — millions of articles made available to anyone, anywhere, at any time. However, because of the enormous amount of content the platform contains, navigating that information can sometimes feel visually overwhelming, especially for users trying to quickly locate topics, categories, or related subjects. ​​​​​​

The goal of this project was not to reinvent Wikipedia's identity, but to explore how its experience could feel more modern, visually structured, and easier to navigate while still preserving the familiarity users associate with the platform. I wanted the redesign to maintain the feeling of open knowledge and accessibility while improving hierarchy, readability, and organization across the homepage, category pages, and article layouts. The concept focused on transforming a dense information system into an experience that feels cleaner, more approachable, and more engaging without losing the academic credibility that defines Wikipedia.

Problem Statement

Wikipedia contains an enormous amount of information, but the presentation of that information can often feel difficult to process because of dense layouts, limited visual separation, and heavy text concentration. Users searching for knowledge may quickly find what they need, but navigating deeper into topics, related categories, and supporting information can become visually exhausting over time.​​​​​​​

For this project, the challenge was to redesign the browsing experience in a way that improved usability without compromising the core purpose of the platform. The interface needed to organize large amounts of information more effectively while preserving Wikipedia's identity as a trusted educational resource. The redesign also needed to support different user behaviors — from casual browsing to deep research — while creating a more visually inviting experience that encourages exploration instead of overwhelming the user with content immediately.

Process

I began by studying Wikipedia's official platform and analyzing how users move between the homepage, category navigation, and article pages. One of the biggest observations was how information-rich the experience is at every stage. While that depth is one of Wikipedia's greatest strengths, it also creates opportunities to improve readability, hierarchy, and visual pacing.​​​​​​​

The wireframing process focused on restructuring the experience into clearer content sections that guide users naturally through discovery and research. For the homepage, I emphasized balance and organization by separating major content areas such as featured articles, news, images, and language access into distinct visual containers. This approach was intended to make the platform feel less crowded while still showcasing the breadth of information available.

For category and navigation pages, I explored ways to simplify large topic systems into layouts that feel easier to scan and understand. Sections such as mathematics, sciences, and overview categories were reorganized using spacing, grouping, and hierarchy to reduce visual fatigue and improve discoverability. I also introduced stronger visual separation between navigation tools, content areas, and supporting resources so users could process information more comfortably during longer browsing sessions.

The article pages themselves were redesigned with readability as a primary focus. I paid close attention to spacing, typography, and content alignment to help long-form educational material feel more approachable. Side panels, related information, and supporting visuals were repositioned to complement the reading experience rather than compete with it. Throughout the process, I worked to maintain Wikipedia’s recognizable structure while introducing a more modern and visually refined presentation style.

Result

The final result is a Wikipedia-inspired interface redesign that creates a cleaner and more structured knowledge-browsing experience without losing the familiarity and trust associated with the original platform. The redesigned layouts improve readability, strengthen content hierarchy, and make navigation across articles and categories feel more intuitive and visually organized.​

By separating large amounts of information into clearer sections and emphasizing spacing, balance, and accessibility, the project transforms the experience from something purely functional into something more engaging and user-centered. The interface encourages exploration while still supporting the fast access to information that users expect from Wikipedia.

More importantly, the project demonstrates my ability to work with information-heavy systems and redesign them in a way that improves usability without sacrificing purpose. Rather than relying on excessive visual effects or dramatic changes, the redesign focuses on thoughtful UX decisions that make knowledge feel easier to explore, absorb, and interact with in a modern digital environment.
